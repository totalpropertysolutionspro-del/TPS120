Why Commercial Kitchen Cleaning Is Different From Regular Cleaning

A restaurant kitchen isn't an office with a bigger sink. It has grease that aerosolizes and settles on every surface, drains that clog with fat, floors that turn slick with a film you can't mop away, and hood systems that trap flammable residue. Standard janitorial cleaning doesn't touch any of that. Commercial kitchen cleaning requires:

  • Degreasing agents rated for food-service use — not generic floor soap
  • Hood, filter, and exhaust awareness — the areas most operators overlook until it's a fire risk
  • Floor and drain care — degreasing floors and clearing drain lines so you're not standing in a slip hazard
  • Health-code knowledge — knowing what NY State and local Albany County inspectors actually look for

That's why "we also do restaurants" from a general cleaner is a red flag. You want a crew that knows food-service compliance, not one that just brings a mop.

Health Inspection Readiness — What Albany Inspectors Look For

New York State and Albany County health inspections score kitchens on cleanliness, food handling, and facility condition. The cleaning-related violations that show up again and again are preventable with a schedule:

  • Grease and food residue on non-food-contact surfaces — equipment, walls, shelving
  • Soiled floors, walls, and ceilings — especially behind and under equipment
  • Clogged or slow drains — a sanitation red flag
  • Improper waste and grease handling — buildup around dumpster areas and grease traps

You can't deep clean your way out of every violation the night before an inspection, but you can remove the standing grime that turns a "pass" into a "conditional."
