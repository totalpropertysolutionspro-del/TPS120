Phase 1: The Rough Clean

This is the unglamorous pass that happens before anyone thinks about making the place shine. We haul out leftover material, sweep and vacuum construction dust, remove stickers and protective film, scrape paint and drywall compound off floors, and knock down the obvious mess. A rough clean alone won't make the space "ready" — but it sets up the final pass so it's actually possible.

Rough cleans run $0.10 to $0.25 per square foot. If a quote comes in below that, someone's skipping the debris haul or counting on your dumpster being free labor.

Phase 2: The Final Detail Clean

This is where the space goes from "construction site" to "walk-through ready." Every surface gets hit: cabinets inside and out, countertops, fixtures, interior windows and glass, baseboards, doors and frames, light switches, vents, and floors — scrubbed, polished, and detailed. The goal is a room that survives a picky client running a white glove over the window sill.

Final detail cleans run $0.15 to $0.35 per square foot. This is the pass people try to skip to save money — and it's the pass that determines whether you get the callback or the complaint.

Phase 3: Inspection-Ready Punch-List Clean

The smart money adds a punch-list pass: after the client or inspector walks the space and flags the missed smudges and haze, we come back and knock out the list. Most contractors build this into the quote up front so there's no second invoice surprise. Ask for it — a cleaner who won't do a punch-list pass is a cleaner who plans to leave you holding the touch-ups.

What Actually Drives the Price

  • Square footage. The baseline. More space, more labor hours, period.
  • Condition. Heavy drywall dust and adhesive residue add time. A light cosmetic remodel cleans faster than a full gut.
  • Ceilings and windows. High ceilings, skylights, and floor-to-ceiling glass need ladders, lifts, and extra passes. Interior-only windows are one rate; exterior adds another.
  • Post-construction extras. Floor stripping and waxing, carpet shampoo, or deep cleaning after the build-out are separate line items — and worth bundling.
  • Timing. Need it done in 48 hours before a grand opening? Rush pricing applies. Booked ahead, you pay standard rates.

Red Flags: How to Spot a Bad Post-Construction Quote

  • A flat "per job" number with no scope. If they won't tell you what's included, they're leaving themselves room to charge you for the "extras" later.
  • No walkthrough, instant quote. Post-construction scope varies wildly. Anyone quoting blind is guessing — or lowballing to win the job and upselling later.
  • No punch-list pass offered. That's how you end up paying a second invoice for touch-ups a week later.
  • Uninsured crew. Post-construction sites have debris, ladders, and liability. If they're not insured and someone gets hurt, that's on you.

How to Get the Best Price on Post-Construction Cleaning

  1. Bundle rough + final + punch list. A single full-scope quote beats three separate invoices — and locks the per-sq-ft rate in lower.
  2. Book ahead. Contractors who line up the clean while the job is still underway pay standard rates and get priority scheduling.
  3. Ask about repeat-project discounts. If you're a contractor doing this monthly, you should have a negotiated rate. Contractor-trusted cleanup should come with contractor pricing.
  4. Bundle the handoff. After the build-out, transition to ongoing janitorial services with the same crew — you skip the learning curve and often lock a better monthly rate.
