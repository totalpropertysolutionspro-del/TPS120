Where Landlords Lose Money on Turnovers

The cleaning bill is the cheapest part of a turnover. The expensive part is everything that happens when the clean goes wrong:

  • Lost rent from vacancy creep. At $45/day, an extra week of vacancy is $315 gone — usually more than the entire cleaning cost.
  • Marketing a dirty unit. You can't schedule showings until the place is presentable. A slow clean pushes your listing window out and drags the next lease signing with it.
  • Re-work. If a tenant or inspector flags missed spots, you pay to fix it — or worse, the new tenant moves in already unhappy.
  • Missed inspection deadlines. Deposit disputes and move-out inspections have hard dates. A no-show cleaner puts you in a corner.
"Landlords don't lose money on cleaning. They lose money on the days a unit sits empty because the clean wasn't done right or wasn't done on time."

What a Make-Ready Clean Actually Covers

A real make-ready cleaning is not a quick sweep. It's a full reset that gets the unit to the standard where a prospective tenant says "yes" on the spot. Here's what's included in a proper turnover clean:

  • Kitchen deep clean — inside and outside of appliances (fridge, oven, microwave, dishwasher), cabinets wiped, counters disinfected, sink and fixtures polished
  • Bathroom full disinfection — tub, shower, toilet, vanity, mirror, and grout; fresh caulk check; restocked toilet paper
  • Floors throughout — vacuum, mop, and edge detailing on all flooring types
  • Walls, baseboards & light fixtures — spot-cleaning of scuffs, dusting of all horizontal surfaces, ceiling fans, and vents
  • Interior windows & sills — streak-free glass and clean tracks
  • Trash & debris removal — nothing left behind for the next tenant
  • Final walkthrough — a documented inspection against a landlord-ready checklist before we call it done
