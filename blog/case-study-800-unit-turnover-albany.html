The Problem: 800 Units, Two Weeks, Zero Margin for Error

Student turnover isn't a cleaning job — it's a logistics operation with a hard deadline. Every unit needs a walkthrough, a deep clean, maintenance follow-up, and a final inspection before the next lease starts. When you're running 800 of them at once, the math gets brutal fast: dozens of buildings, hundreds of move-outs landing the same weekend, and owners who expect their units rent-ready the moment the last tenant walks out.

Miss the deadline and you're looking at empty units during peak rental season, angry owners, and a reputation hit that costs you next year's leases. There is no "we'll get to it next week." The calendar doesn't care.

Why the "12 Vendors" Approach Fails

The default move is to piece it together: a cleaning company here, a handyman there, a painter, a trash hauler, a carpet guy. Before you know it, you're juggling a dozen vendors across 800 units. Here's what that actually produces:

  • Twelve schedules to coordinate — one no-show cascades into a missed move-in.
  • Twelve quality standards — "clean" means something different to every vendor, so every unit looks different.
  • Twelve points of failure — and when a unit fails inspection, everyone points fingers and nobody fixes it.
  • Zero accountability — the cleaner blames the painter, the painter blames the schedule, and you eat the vacancy cost.

The problem isn't the individual vendors. It's that no single person owns the outcome. That's the gap TPS Pro closes.

The Playbook: How We Turn 800 Units in One Cycle

Here's the exact system we ran — the same one we use for every student housing turnover we take on:

  1. Pre-season audit (two weeks out). We walk every unit, log its condition, and build a cleaning + maintenance scope for each one. No surprises on move-out day.
  2. Zone-based scheduling. We assign crews by building and by zone, not by "whoever's free." Units get cleaned in the order move-ins arrive — earliest move-in first.
  3. One standardized make-ready spec. Every unit is cleaned to the same documented standard, so 800 units look consistent, not like 800 different vendors touched them.
  4. Parallel cleaning + maintenance. While crews clean, our maintenance coordination handles punch-list items — paint touch-ups, plumbing, appliance checks — so units aren't sitting idle waiting on two different schedules.
  5. QC with photo proof. A supervisor spot-checks every job and we document it digitally. You know exactly what was done before your owner asks.

The result is a turnover that runs like an assembly line — without sacrificing the detail that passes a landlord inspection the first time.

What Albany Property Managers Can Steal From This (Even If They Don't Hire Us)

We're putting this out there on purpose. Here's what you can apply tomorrow:

  • Do a pre-season walkthrough and log every unit's needs before move-out day — surprises are what kill timelines.
  • Schedule by zone and move-in date, never "whenever." Lose control of the calendar and you lose control of the turnover.
  • Demand a standardized scope of work from whoever cleans — verbal "do a good job" doesn't scale.
  • Require photo documentation. If your vendor can't show you what was done, assume it wasn't.
  • Consolidate vendors. Fewer points of failure = fewer missed deadlines. One partner owning the full turn beats twelve vendors pointing fingers.
