Albany ROP by the Numbers:

Permit valid for 30 months · $50 per unit registration fee (covers first inspection + one re-inspection) · $50/unit for each additional inspection · 30 days to fix non-critical violations · Renting without a valid ROP is a violation of Albany City Code Chapter 231.

What Is an ROP (Residential Occupancy Permit)?

Under Albany City Code Chapter 231, every residential rental dwelling unit must be registered on the city's Rental Dwelling Registry and certified with a Residential Occupancy Permit. The permit is issued only after a Code Enforcement Officer inspects the unit and confirms it meets city code. It's not optional paperwork — it's the legal green light to rent.

The permit is valid for 30 months from the date of issuance. When it expires, you must re-register and pass a new inspection. There's no grandfather clause and no automatic renewal. It's on you to track the clock.

⚠️ The Cost of Skipping It

Renting, leasing, or even allowing occupancy of a unit without a valid ROP is unlawful under Chapter 231. That means fines, a halted rental, and a unit you can't legally collect rent on. For most landlords, the $50 fee is the cheapest part of the whole process — the real risk is the vacancy and legal exposure from non-compliance.

The 3-Step ROP Process (And What It Actually Costs)

  1. Register. Submit a Rental Dwelling Registration Form to the Department of Buildings & Regulatory Compliance and pay the $50-per-unit fee. No inspection is scheduled until that fee is paid.
  2. Schedule the inspection. A Code Enforcement Officer walks the unit. Give yourself at least 10 days of lead time for form processing.
  3. Pass. Once you pass, the ROP is mailed the following week. Fail, and you get a list of violations plus time to fix them (30 days for non-critical items).

Your $50 covers the initial inspection and one re-inspection. Blow the re-inspection, and it's another $50 per unit per attempt. Cancel within 24 hours or no-show? That's $50 too. The cheapest ROP is the one you pass on the first try.

The Inspection Checklist — What the Officer Actually Checks

This is the part that catches landlords off guard, because several line items are cleaning and maintenance issues — not legal paperwork:

  • Smoke & CO detectors: Working smoke detectors in every sleeping room, the corridor outside sleeping rooms, outside any bathroom with a shower, near cooking appliances, and at least one per floor. CO detectors in sleeping rooms, corridors, and near fuel-burning appliances.
  • Clear egress: Two ways out of the unit, properly lit, clear of debris, and no key-locked doors blocking the exit from inside.
  • General cleanliness: The unit must be free of rubbish, garbage, and hazardous materials — and free of animal infestation. This is a literal inspection criterion.
  • Security: Lockable doors in working order, plus optical viewers (peepholes) on unit doors.
  • Structure: Foundation and structural members sound, no signs of failure.
  • Electrical: Covered outlets and switches, no exposed wiring, no extension cords used as permanent wiring.
  • Clean running water: The unit needs functioning, clean water.

Notice how many of these are exactly what a proper make-ready clean covers. That's not a coincidence — the city is checking that your unit is genuinely habitable, not just that you filled out a form.

Key Deadlines & Fine Print Worth Knowing

  • Renew every 30 months — or sooner if ownership or registration info changes (re-register within 30 days of any change).
  • New owner? Re-register within 30 days of taking ownership. The existing ROP stays valid until it expires.
  • Fee exemption: Owner-occupied dwellings with no more than one rental unit, owned by owners 65+, are exempt from the $50 fee only (still must register).
  • No penalties for ROP inspection violations — but you must fix them to get the permit, and re-inspections cost money.

Get the current forms and fee schedule straight from the city at albanyny.gov before you file — codes change, and the last thing you want is a surprise fee on inspection day.
