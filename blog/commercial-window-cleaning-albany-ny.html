What "Commercial Window Cleaning" Actually Includes

It's more than a squeegee and a bucket. A proper commercial window cleaning service covers the full glass package — and it should be done by the same accountable partner, not farmed out:

  • Interior and exterior glass — both sides of every pane, because cleaning one side still leaves the other one dull.
  • Storefront windows and entry doors — the first thing a customer sees, and the first thing to collect fingerprints and grime.
  • Interior glass partitions and office fronts — conference rooms, lobby glass, and cubicle dividers that collect smudges daily.
  • Frames, tracks, and sills — the buildup that makes even clean glass look neglected when it's ignored.
  • Post-construction glass — the film, adhesive, and paint overspray left behind after a build or renovation. See our post-construction cleanup page for the full scope.
  • High-reach and second-story work — done safely with the right equipment and training, not a guy on a wobbly ladder.

Why Albany Is Harder on Glass Than You Think

The Capital Region does a number on windows. Road salt and ice melt kick up all winter and coat storefronts in a crusty white film. Hard water from the region leaves mineral spots that bake on in the sun. Summer pollen, fall rain, and year-round construction dust all stack up on glass — and once a film forms, plain soap and water won't cut it.

That's why professional-grade window cleaning uses purified water and proper technique, not a spray bottle from the supply closet. It's also why commercial cleaning in Albany NY should include window care as part of a consistent schedule, not a once-a-year scramble before a big event.

How Often Should You Clean Commercial Windows?

Frequency depends on what the glass is selling for you:

  • Retail and storefronts: monthly. Your window is your ad. Keep it clean.
  • Restaurants and cafes: monthly, plus a spot-clean of entry glass weekly — fingerprints and grease buildup fast.
  • Offices and professional practices: every 1–3 months, depending on traffic and exterior exposure.
  • Medical and dental offices: monthly — a clean, bright waiting room builds trust before a patient sits down.
  • Multi-unit and property-managed buildings: quarterly exteriors plus common-area glass on a janitorial schedule.
