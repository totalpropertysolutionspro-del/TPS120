Albany Property Management by the Numbers:

Most Albany-area property managers charge 8–12% of monthly rent for a single-family or small multi-unit portfolio, plus a leasing fee of 50–100% of one month's rent. On a $1,500/month unit, that's $1,440–$2,160 a year in management fees — before a single repair or turnover.

The Three Ways Albany Property Managers Charge

There's no single "standard" price. Most companies use one of three structures — and the structure you choose changes your annual cost more than the headline rate does.

1. Percentage of Monthly Rent (Most Common)

You pay a percentage of collected rent every month — typically 8–12% in the Albany market for residential portfolios under 50 units. Larger portfolios often negotiate down to 6–8%. The catch: you pay this whether the unit is full or vacant, and some companies keep charging a reduced "vacancy fee" even when there's no tenant.

2. Flat Monthly Fee

Some managers charge a flat rate — commonly $100–$150 per unit per month for single-family homes. This is predictable, which landlords like, but watch the fine print: flat-fee plans often exclude maintenance coordination, inspections, and after-hours emergency calls, then bill those separately.

3. À La Carte / Build-Your-Own

You pay a base fee plus add-ons: tenant placement, leasing, inspections, maintenance dispatch, and turnover services. This looks cheapest on paper until you add up the line items. À la carte is where the hidden fees live.

The Hidden Fees Most Landlords Don't See Coming

Here's where property management companies make their real margin — and where unsuspecting landlords get burned:

  • Leasing / placement fee: 50–100% of the first month's rent, charged every time you re-tenant a unit.
  • Setup / onboarding fee: $250–$500 to "open your account" — often pure profit.
  • Maintenance markup: 10–20% added on top of every contractor invoice. A $500 repair quietly becomes $600.
  • Turnover / make-ready upcharge: cleaning and painting between tenants, marked up 15–30% over the actual cost.
  • Vacancy fee: a reduced monthly charge even while the unit sits empty.
  • Lease renewal fee: $100–$250 just to renew an existing tenant's lease.

The worst part? Turnover is where landlords lose the most money — a vacant unit costs you rent every single day it sits dirty and unshowable. Yet most managers treat turnover as an afterthought, then mark it up when they do handle it. That's the gap TPS Pro was built to close.

How to Compare Property Management Quotes (7 Questions to Ask)

Before you sign anything, get every fee in writing and ask these seven questions:

  1. What's the exact percentage or flat fee, and what does it include?
  2. What are ALL the additional fees — leasing, renewal, setup, inspection, vacancy, maintenance markup?
  3. Is there a maintenance markup, and how much? Will you see original invoices?
  4. How is turnover handled — and is cleaning marked up?
  5. What's your average vacancy time and turnover turnaround? A slow turnover is the hidden cost most people never calculate.
  6. Can you bundle cleaning and management to eliminate vendor markups? (Only one Albany company will say yes.)
  7. What's the exit clause? Can you leave without penalty if the numbers don't work?

Get all of it in writing. If a company hesitates to itemize their fees, that's your answer — walk away.
